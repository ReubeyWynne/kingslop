Control the castle · understand the fight

Castle Battle

Taking the castle wins a fight. Holding it wins the event.

Learn the objective first, then the combat behind your rallies and garrison. Hero examples cover Generations 1–3; the event and combat lessons apply beyond them.

Fight for occupation time

The battlefield has a central King's Castle and four surrounding turrets. The castle is the objective; turrets put pressure on its occupants.

Current support rules describe a five-hour battle, with an early win at 2½ hours of occupation. The Kingdom of Power FAQ explicitly requires that early hold to be consecutive. Otherwise, the longest occupation wins when time expires. Read your event's Rules and live occupation timer before the battle: older guides still quote six hours / three hours. Event sources

Personal battle points are a separate reward objective. A high score does not replace the occupation requirement.

Prepare
Agree who takes the castle, who holds each turret, who leads, and who stays available to reinforce. Save separate rally and garrison presets. Check your infirmary space and healing resources.
Take
Scout the current occupant. Fill the assigned rally with the requested troop types and joiner heroes. Choose an arrival time and a plan for the garrison immediately after capture.
Hold
Check the active garrison bonuses and hero skills. Watch incoming marches, turret control, remaining infantry and available capacity. Refill what is missing.
Retake
After losing control, read the new garrison rather than repeating the last attack blindly. The opponent may have different troops, a different bonus source and fresh reinforcements.

Turrets change the cost of holding

A turret attacks the castle occupants when they are held by different teams. It does not attack its own team's castle garrison. Its firing interval shortens as occupation continues. Taking hostile turrets relieves pressure; taking one while attacking adds pressure. That is why castle and turret assignments belong in the same plan. Castle rules

Local Castle Battle and KvK

Local Castle Battle is an alliance contest within your kingdom. KvK puts the castle contest inside Kingdom of Power, with a preparation phase and cross-kingdom battle rules. Follow the current KvK event screen for battlefield access, kingdom ownership and turret bonuses; don't import a local alliance plan unchanged.

Coordinate arrivals, not a “wave” label

Several rallies are separate fights. Bringing their arrivals close together can leave less time to refill between hits; it does not merge them into one army or guarantee capture. After capture, the next attack faces the new occupant.

Arrival = rally start + assembly timer + march time

Both sides deal damage

Expedition combat resolves in simultaneous turns. Each side selects targets and attacks; losses are applied before the next turn. The fight ends when one side has no active troops left. Your rally needs both damage and enough survival time to deliver it. Combat FAQ

1 · InfantryThe frontline. Takes normal attacks first.
2 · CavalryThe next row when infantry are gone. Can bypass to archers through its troop skill.
3 · ArchersHigh damage, fragile when exposed. Normal attacks reach this row last.

All surviving rows attack; back rows do not wait for infantry to die. A counter bonus affects damage to the target actually hit. Sending cavalry does not make every cavalry attack hit archers. Hero skills can also change targeting.

The four stats work in pairs

In the Frakinator model already used by this site's simulator, offensive strength scales with attack × lethality, and resistance with defense × health. These apply on both sides. Lethality is not a special “permanent kills” stat; health and defense help an attacking rally too. Model and provenance

Offense: base attack × (1 + ATK/100) × (1 + LET/100)
Resistance: base health × (1 + DEF/100) × (1 + HP/100)

Why the lower paired stat can be a better upgrade

With 500% ATK and 100% LET, the bonus factors multiply to 6 × 2 = 12. Adding 100 percentage points of ATK gives 7 × 2 = 14. Adding them to LET gives 6 × 3 = 18. Compare the relative gain and upgrade cost, rather than choosing by the stat name.

The model uses the square root of each troop type's active count in its damage calculation. Twice the archers does not mean twice their per-turn damage. Troops still matter: they also give you more losses to absorb. Hero effects and chance skills sit on top of the basic calculation.

A hero label does not decide its job

Squad-wide Expedition skills still work without matching troops. An archer hero can support an infantry-heavy army. The actual scope of the skill decides which troops benefit; the hero's troop icon does not prescribe your ratio. Widget skills can require rally or defender duty. Ordinary skills that improve survival can help in either role.

Read the report in the right order

  1. Compare starting counts, tiers and Truegold levels.
  2. Check whose bonuses were used, and the per-type ATK, LET, DEF and HP.
  3. Check leader heroes, widgets and the four selected joiner skills. Hero effects are not fully explained by the displayed bonus percentages.
  4. Look at which troop types were removed and what remains. Then check the live garrison for reinforcements before planning another hit.

For King's Castle fights, support says casualties enter the infirmary until it fills, then further casualties die. Keep healing and watch capacity. Removing troops from a battle and permanently killing them are different outcomes. City attacks have different casualty rules. Casualty rules

Your troops matter. Your full kit does not transfer.

The attacking leader supplies the rally's account bonuses and full leader kit. Joiners supply troops, including their type and tier, and up to four selected first-hero skills are shared across the rally. Your own gear, charms and combat research do not add another set of rally bonuses.

Put the assigned hero in slot 1, the captain slot. Its first Expedition skill is the eligible contribution. Your other heroes can help deployment capacity; their skills, and skill 2 or 3 of the first hero, do not transfer. Check the rally's selected skills before it marches. Sloth's leader / joiner explanation

PvP needs survival on attack too

Bear never attacks you. A castle garrison does. Gordon, Saul, Fahd and Eric have useful survival contributions in PvP; an offensive rally does not automatically need four Bear joiners. Chenko and Amane add damage support, but selecting only damage skills can shorten the time your army has to use it.

Coordinate the four effects together. Four Gordons are a simple early health-stacking option. Saul splits defense and health; Fahd and Eric provide different enemy debuffs. Mixing effect families can improve the combined result, depending on the leader kit. Don't rank a hero solely by the biggest percentage in its tooltip.

Four skill slots, not four troop marches

Members outside those four skill slots still supply troops. A fifth Gordon is not a fifth health contribution. Eligibility, which four skills are selected, and whether their effects stack are separate checks.

Max the assigned first skill and inspect the actual selection in the rally window. Don't assume the game picks the most useful four heroes. An unassigned hero can occupy a slot you needed for something else. If you cannot supply the requested hero, ask the lead whether to send troops without heroes.

Why four copies are not always four effects

Sloth's testing distinguishes reliable stat contributions from chance-based skills that do not stack across identical copies. Gordon can stack; multiple Petras or Marlins do not provide four independent copies of their first-skill benefit. A single Petra can still be useful when she is not already leading. Check the specific skill interaction rather than applying one blanket rule to every hero. Petra analysis · Marlin analysis

Garrisons have an extra trap

The first occupant is not necessarily the source of the garrison's bonuses. The game selects the member with the highest stat bonuses. Keep the intended strong defender present, inspect the active bonuses and hero skills, and check again when membership changes. Don't assume that the player shown as captain explains every part of the defense. Sloth: overlapping defensive roles

Follow the lead's troop request. Your march does not need the same ratio as every other joiner: it contributes to the combined army. A refill request for infantry means infantry, even if your saved preset includes all three types.

A ratio is a tradeoff, not a combat rating

More infantry buys frontline durability but occupies spaces you could give to damage troops. Too little infantry exposes fragile rows sooner. Too much can leave a durable army that cannot clear the opponent. Cavalry adds damage and bypass pressure; archers add strong damage against the infantry they normally face.

Start from a lead's tested setup for your generation and opponent. Change it using actual reports: troop tiers, bonuses, hero skills and the opposing composition all matter. Neither 50 / 20 / 30 nor 60 / 20 / 20 is a proven universal answer.

What about infantry / cavalry with no archers?

Later-generation skills can change the tradeoff. Sloth discusses the infantry / cavalry 60 / 40 garrison setup and Saul's renewed role in Generations 4–6. That is a different hero context from the Gen 2–3 examples here, not a reason to apply 60 / 40 everywhere. Saul and the later garrison setup

Find the missing troops

A garrison's ratio changes after losses. Repeating the opening mixed preset may fill capacity while leaving it short of infantry. Compare current counts with your lead's intended counts.

Surplus troops cannot be fixed by adding more troops into a full garrison. The lead must decide whether and when to recall or replace marches. Account for troops already on the way, and don't withdraw a key defender during an incoming attack.

Test a change you can explain

  1. Record both sides' current troop counts, tiers, bonuses and hero setup.
  2. Change one thing: the combined troop split, a leader hero, or the selected joiner mix.
  3. Compare repeated reports. Chance skills can change a single outcome.
  4. Judge capture and troops remaining for the next defense, alongside healing cost and occupation time.
