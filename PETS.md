# Pets

The Pets page (`pets/`) lists the fourteen pets of the Beast Cage, records yours in the shared ledger, counts what the next Advance costs and scores, and lets you look after the ones you have tamed: each one gets hungry and lonely in real time.

## Data

`_data/pets.json` is generated by `.dsh/build-pets.py` from the progression research (`research/progression/data/pets.json` in the project files). Do not edit it by hand. It holds:

- `pets`: id, name, generation, `unlockDay` (Kingshot Data's day for the generation, about), `maxLevel`, `profile`, `advances` (one per ten levels) and the skill: its name, the in-game description at the top level (`say`) and the ladder, one value per Advance;
- `profiles`: the five cost profiles every pet in a generation shares, each level with `petFood` (the price of reaching it), `advance` (the extra price of breaking its cap) and `score` (its Pet Advancement score), plus `totals` from level 1 to max;
- `points`: KvK / Strongest Governor and Brawl points per Advance (score × 50 and × 30).

The three sources agree on every row but three cells, where the two that agree win (`research/progression/pets.md`). Ironclad War Bear has no KS h5joy page, so it is `verified: false` and its dossier says so. The passive attack and defence bonus (one source) and refinement (the guides disagree on the tiers; no roll odds) are left out until they are checked in game.

## Advances

`js/pet-plan.js` (`PetPlan`) works from a pet's level and whether the Advance at that level is done. The skill level is the number of Advances done. `nextAdvance` is the cost of the next cap with its Advance, `toMax` the cost of every step left; both carry the Advances' score and points. An unread level counts as nothing.

## Care

`js/pet-care.js` (`PetCare`) keeps two meters per tamed pet, fullness and affection, as a value and the time it was set; the current value is worked out from the clock, so pets get hungry (empty over 16 hours) and lonely (over 10) whether or not the page is open. Pet Food adds 30, a pat 20, and a pet over 90 full turns food down. The mood follows the whole-point meters: asleep at night (23:00–06:00 local) after ten minutes alone, then hungry (below 25), lonely (below 25), delighted (both 70 or more), content (average 45 or more), else restless. The care lives under `ks.pet-care.v1` in local storage, not in the ledger, so it never reaches saves or the event maths; an unreadable record starts again.

## Page

- `js/pet-den.js` registers `pet-den` (pens, care, the "Your pet" form and costs) and `pet-advances` (the KvK points table). It sets `data-mood` and, for a moment after an action, `data-react` on each pen; CSS does all of the drawing. Moods are recomputed on load, after each action, once a minute and when the tab returns.
- The page explains none of the care; players find it by hand. A tamed pet is covered by a clear button (`.pet-stroke`): three turns of rubbing back and forth over it, or half its width of stroking with a finger or a held button, is a pat, and keeping on counts again. It takes `touch-action: pan-y`, so a vertical swipe still scrolls. Feeding is dragging the `@` out of the bowl under the pen and letting it go over the pet; it steps a whole character cell at a time (`--cx`, `--cy`), and the pet squints at it (`data-sniff`) while it is held over the pen. A plain click does neither; from the keyboard, Enter on the pen pets and Enter on the food feeds. The pen's accessible name carries the mood.
- Every dossier is a `details` with `data-deep-link="pet-{id}"`, so `#pet-lion` opens the Lion's.
- The pens are the Pets scene (`pets()` in `.dsh/build-ascii.py`): up, low, happy and asleep bodies per pet in a 44 x 15 pen and a shared layer of marks. The happy body (eyes squeezed shut, a quick wag) shows while a pet is petted, fed or offered food. A delighted pet wags at 1.2s a loop, a content one at 2.8s (`css/ascii.css`). The Gray Wolf, delighted, is the page hero and deck cover.
- A pet can instead be drawn from a low-poly model as a coloured portrait, and now every pet is. The Mighty Bison, Regal White Lion, Great Moose and Ironclad War Bear reuse their base pet's model with their own inks and a small `shape` change (bigger horns or antlers, smaller ears) that `render.cjs` and `mesh.cjs` bend into the rest pose. GLB models are flattened to STL first (mesh-space triangles, bind pose); the GLB sources and their licences are kept in the project files under `pets/models/sources`, and the models that ask for credit are credited under Sources on the page. `.dsh/pet-portrait/shade.py` renders the model offline (`render.cjs`, three.js in headless Chromium) through a text shader: the light on each character cell picks its glyph, the fur under it times that light picks one of seven inks, and silhouette and depth edges get line glyphs. Each pose is the model bent a little (an ear tipped, the nose flared, the head raised or lowered) through one camera; the baked frames live in `.dsh/pet-portraits/<pet>.json`, and `portraits()` in `.dsh/build-ascii.py` turns them into one stepped span per ink: a blink, ear flick and sniff when content, a lowered head and slow blink when low, head down with eyes shut asleep, and happy `^` eyes with an ear flick while petted or fed. The pen gives those pets their ink spans (`portraits` in `pets/index.html`) and their tones (`css/ascii.css`). To re-bake: `npm i --no-save three@0.169.0`, then `python3 .dsh/pet-portrait/shade.py grizzly-bear path/to/model.stl`, then `python3 .dsh/build-ascii.py`. Every pet's settings are one entry in `PETS` in `shade.py` (orientation, inks, fur regions, the face's points, the skeleton's pivots and weights, and the camera's framing); their models are in the project files at `pets/models/<pet>.stl`.
- Where WebGL 2 runs, these pets are drawn live instead (`js/pet-live.js`; DESIGN.md, "Live pets"). Each model is `pets/models/<pet>.bin`, about 9 KB: the STL turned to stand up and face forward, simplified to 1,000 triangles and framed like `shade.py` (`shade.py` runs `node .dsh/pet-portrait/mesh.cjs <pet> path/to/model.stl`; it needs `npm i --no-save meshoptimizer@0.22.0`), beside `pets/models/<pet>.json`, which `shade.py` writes with the pet's inks, fur regions, face points, skeleton and camera. The script paints the skeleton's weights from each vertex's rest position, so they cost nothing to download, and takes everything else from the pen: `data-mood` sets the posture (content tilts its head, delighted perks its ears, restless glances about, lonely and hungry droop with ears back and worried brows, asleep breathes with its head tipped and z's rising), `data-react` plays the moment (petted: `^ ^`, blush and hearts; fed: chewing; full: it turns its nose up), and `data-sniff` makes it sniff at the food. An untamed pet is drawn in the pen's dim colour. When the canvas is up the pen has `data-live`, and `css/pets.css` hides the baked portrait and the shared marks under it. Adding a pet means an entry in `PETS` in `shade.py`, one run of it, the pet in `portraits` in `pets/index.html` and its tones in `css/ascii.css`; the script needs no change.

## Checks

- `.dsh/pet-care-check.cjs`: data against the research totals, Advance costs and points, care meters and moods, the ledger section, the generated pens, navigation and dictionaries.
- `.dsh/pets-ui-check.cjs`: the page in a browser with a controlled clock (taming, rubbing and dragging food, keyboard care, decay, sleep, reactions, the Advances table), every language at five widths, and the no-JavaScript fallback; and the live pets in a WebGL browser (it covers its pen, draws, and stays off with reduced motion).
