BEAR HUNT,
DEMYSTIFIED

How rally damage works in plain words — and the four things you should do about it.

30 min a run · every 48 hours · Forgehammer goldmine · F2P friendly

The one-liner

Remember one sentence and you basically know the whole event:

The lead sets the power. You supply the troops. Each troop type gets its own square root.

THE LEAD Sets the power

Everyone in the rally uses the lead's combat stats and full hero kit. Up to four locked joiner first-skills become shared rally skills too.

YOU Supply the troops

As a joiner, you contribute the counts and tiers of your infantry, cavalry and archers. Those three troop types are scored as separate square-root buckets.

Your own attack, lethality, gear and buffs do not follow your troops into somebody else's Bear rally.

The square root

The sqrt is the whole game. Here's the only number you need: double your march → +41%, not +100%.

Same troop type. Just split across more marches:

1 march
1.0×
2 marches
1.4×
4 marches
2.0×
6 marches
2.4×
  • 2× the troops = +41%, not double
  • 4× the troops = 2.0×
  • 16× the troops = only 4.0×

First troops hit hardest. Every extra troop hits a little softer than the last. So bigger marches help a little — more marches help a lot.

The exact maths

Per player, per rally, damage is added over your three troop types:

Σ  baset × At(lead) × √(nt)

  • nt — troops of type t in your march.
  • At(lead) = (1 + atk%) × (1 + lethality%) of the lead, per type. Your own attack & lethality never enter it.
  • baset — the relative weights at T6: infantry ⅓, cavalry 1, archers 4.4/3 ≈ 1.47. The archer weight is (4/3) × 1.1 — the bear is all infantry and archers do +10% vs infantry. At T7+/TG3+ archers pick up another 1.1, so the weight becomes 4.84/3 ≈ 1.61.

The full form also carries the fixed Bear factor (1.2/1000) × √5000, your troops' base attack (higher tier = higher base = more damage), the lead's hero kit, and the rally's four locked joiner skills. Those joiner skills are shared by every participant; contributing one does not give its owner a private bonus. None of that changes the √ and split rules on this page.

Fill the rally

A rally holds 1 lead + up to 14 joiners. The goal is not one huge march — it is 13–14 separate joiner damage buckets whenever possible.

A 900k rally with 15 players → 60k each.

FULL RALLY — 15 players≈ 3.9×

For the same total troops, more players means more independent √ buckets. Oversized join marches can cost an entire player's bucket.

That is why our joiner cap is about 85k: it is a throughput rule, not a fairness rule. Protect the 13th and 14th joiner slots.

TRY IT — FILL THE RALLY

Target average: 60,000 per player · rally hits 3.9× harder than one player carrying the same total troops.

Why, in one line of maths

15 players × √(900k ÷ 15) = √(15 × 900k) ≈ 3.9× one player's √900k. The exact troop mix changes the constants, not the reason extra participant slots are valuable.

Spread your marches

Your join queues are reusable damage buckets. Start by dividing your archers equally across every join queue, then do the same with cavalry and infantry until each queue reaches the cap.

TRY IT — SPLIT YOUR ARCHERS

50,000 archers per queue · those same archers have 2.4× the square-root output of one combined bucket before queue reuse is counted.

Do not save a giant archer march for a random stronger lead. In a live hunt, equal queues + next rally to fire is the strategy you can actually execute.

Why equal queues win

For one troop type, a fixed pool P across Q equivalent queues is maximised when the queues are equal: Q × √(P ÷ Q) = √(Q × P). Six equal buckets = √6 ≈ 2.4× one bucket, before those queues rotate into later rallies.

Keep moving

A rally fires 5 minutes after launch. Join troops usually return fast enough to go again; the practical bottleneck is how many join slots the alliance creates.

The supply equation: hits per join queue = launchers × joiners per rally ÷ participants ÷ join queues.

If everybody launches, participant count cancels out. With 6 join queues and 13–14 joiners per rally, the structural ceiling is only 2.17–2.33 hits per queue every 5 minutes — even if marches could physically return faster.

TRY IT — RALLY THROUGHPUT

That creates 180 join slots per cycle: 1.50× supported hits per join queue · stagger launches about every 20s.

The physical 15–30 second turnaround can make 3–4 hits look possible for one queue. Alliance-wide, it cannot happen unless enough rally slots exist. Rally supply is the cap.

So do not shop for the perfect leader. Stagger launches across the whole five-minute cycle, then join the next suitable rally to fire. Fire, return, take the next one.

The timing, in detail
  • Fire = launch + 5:00, always.
  • If L people launch each cycle, ideal spacing is 300 ÷ L seconds. 15 launchers → 20s; 20 launchers → 15s.
  • With P participants, L launchers, S joiners per rally and Q join queues, supported hits per queue are H = L·S ÷ (P·Q).
  • If everybody launches, L = P, so H = S ÷ Q. More participants add both rally supply and join queues; the ratio cancels.
  • At Q = 6, 12 joiners → 2.00 hits/queue; 13 → 2.17; the absolute 14-joiner ceiling → 2.33.
  • A queue sitting in a fresh five-minute countdown while a near-fire rally has space is wasted dwell time.
  • The easiest alliance-wide gains are therefore more people launching and more joiner slots surviving the cap.

Troop mix

The square root is applied separately to infantry, cavalry and archers. Archers are still the scarce high-damage troop, so build your join queues around them first.

Equalise first

Divide your total archers equally across your join queues. Do the same with cavalry and infantry until the queues fill. The first troops in a √ bucket have the highest marginal value — there is no magical 5k minimum.

Work toward 5 / 15 / 80

As you become troop-rich and each join queue reaches the ~85k cap, converge on roughly 5% infantry / 15% cavalry / 80% archers. At 85k that is 4.25k / 12.75k / 68k.

Strong leads are different

A genuinely outsized rally lead should full-send their own lead march with their calculated ideal ratio. Their six join queues still obey the normal cap and equalisation rule.

5/15/80 is an operational preset, not a claim that every leader's mathematical optimum is identical.

How to build six join queues

At an 85k cap, six 5/15/80 queues hold 25.5k infantry, 76.5k cavalry and 408k archers. Until you reach those amounts, spread what you own evenly rather than forcing the ratio. Once capped, excess infantry and cavalry stay home first; most players should have very few archers left unused.

If you like the maths

For one lead, maximising Σ baset·At·√nt gives ft ∝ (baset·At)². That exact ratio depends on the lead's attack and lethality by troop type. Use the battle simulator for a designated strong lead; use the fixed join preset everywhere else.

Tier & heroes — the fine print
  • Troop tier: damage scales with your troops' base attack — send your highest tier. The weights above are T6-era numbers.
  • T7+ / TG3+: archers gain another ×1.1 vs the bear (all-infantry), weight 4.4/3 → 4.84/3 ≈ 1.61.
  • Heroes: the lead's full hero kit applies to every participant, and up to four joiner first-skills are locked as shared rally skills. The joiner who supplies one gets no private ownership bonus.

The heroes

The lead's full kit and the rally's four locked joiner skills are the last big multiplier left. Two jobs, two lists — and a hero that shines at one is usually dead weight at the other.

How to read this: a joiner's first hero may supply one of the rally's four shared first-skills. If selected, that skill is used for every participant's Bear simulation, not just the player who supplied it. A leader brings their full kit and stats. Same skill codes stack additively; different codes multiply. Defence does nothing to a bear.

The cap

The joiner cap exists to protect participant count. An oversized march is not just using extra troops — it can remove somebody else's entire square-root damage bucket.

The maths of the cap

For a rally with capacity R and a lead march of L, a target of J joiners suggests join cap ≈ (R − L) ÷ J. In practice we use a conservative fixed ~85k so ordinary rallies reliably land around 13–14 joiners.

For the same total joined troops, 14 equal participant buckets have √(14/10) ≈ 18% more square-root output than 10 buckets. Real rallies are messier, but the direction is brutal: protecting slots matters more than squeezing another oversized join march in.

The cap stays on joiner marches. The exception is a designated outsized rally lead: their own lead march can be full-sized and use its calculated ideal ratio. That exception never follows them into somebody else's rally.
