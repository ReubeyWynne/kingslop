BEAR HUNT,
DEMYSTIFIED

One goal: keep as many useful rally slots and march queues scoring as possible.

30 min · every 48 hours · 5-minute rally cycle

The plan

The rally lead supplies the combat stats and full hero kit. Joiners supply their troop counts and tiers. Up to four joiner first-hero skills are shared across the whole rally.

  • Everyone launches. Strong leads are valuable, but extra rallies create extra places for returning join queues to score.
  • Protect rally slots. Keep join marches around 85k so rallies can fit roughly 13–14 joiners.
  • Use every queue. Spread scarce archers instead of building one oversized join march; as queues fill, work toward 5 / 15 / 80.
  • Be strict about joiner heroes. Use an approved first hero, or send no hero. A bad first skill can occupy one of the four shared slots.

Lead quality and rally supply are different levers. A stronger lead makes each slot better; another rally creates more slots. If a march can hit both, the weaker rally is additional damage rather than a replacement for the stronger one.

For each troop type, damage grows roughly with the square root of the number you send. Doubling a bucket gives about 41% more damage, not 100%.

That is why we care about separate players in a rally and separate march queues. The exact multiplier changes with lead strength, troop type, heroes and procs; the diminishing-return shape does not.

Exact model

A useful proportional form for one participant is:

D ∝ KI√I + KC√C + KA√A

The three K values come from the rally lead's attack/lethality package, troop base attack, hero/widget effects and the Bear matchup. Chance-based skills add noise across the ten rounds, so for planning we care about their average effect rather than trying to route every march around a proc.

Aim for 13–14 joiners in every rally. Our current join cap is about 85k troops.

The cap is a throughput rule, not a fairness rule. One oversized join march can consume enough capacity to keep another player out, removing an entire independent damage bucket.

A designated strong lead is the exception only for their own lead march: fill it, do not apply the 85k join cap, and use that lead's tested or simulated ratio. When that player joins somebody else's rally, the normal join cap applies again.

Why 13–14 matters

If the same useful troops are spread over 14 participant buckets instead of 10, the idealised square-root gain is about 18%. Real rallies are not perfectly equal, but losing several participant slots is still expensive.

Use your highest-tier troops. Treat your own rally as another scoring bucket, then keep the join queues as even as practical.

Join marches

Cap at about 85k. As you become troop-rich, work toward 5% infantry / 15% cavalry / 80% archers: about 4.25k / 12.75k / 68k at the cap.

Use a controlled two-wave start. After that, stop scheduling and relaunch as soon as your rally heroes return.

  1. Poll before the event. A short same-day Bear chat poll for availability and Group 1/2 preference is enough. Plan for some non-voters and late arrivals.
  2. Group 1 launches immediately. Size it to the join queues you expect to have at the start, with a small surplus if one or two leads are willing to flex.
  3. Group 2 launches at roughly 28:00–27:30 remaining. Returning Group 1 join marches then have plenty of time to move into those rallies.
  4. Flex if Group 1 is too large. A nominated lead can cancel and relaunch with Group 2 rather than let an opening rally fire half-empty.
  5. After the first rally, relaunch on return. Travel time and human reaction naturally spread later launches. Joiners take the next suitable rally that is close to firing instead of waiting for the perfect lead.

Fast relaunch trick: while your lead march is walking home from the Bear, open the rally screen and keep tapping your saved Bear lead formation. It will not load while the troops are away; the first tap after they arrive loads it immediately, so you can relaunch without waiting to notice the return.

How many rallies belong in Group 1?

Estimate the join queues available at the start and divide by the number of joiners you want per rally. With 15 players and six join queues each, 90 opening join queues support about seven 13-person join groups. Starting eight can still be sensible when one or two leads are ready to cancel if the room does not fill.

Later in the event the same join queue can score again after it returns, so the opening-wave limit is not the steady-state limit.

For join marches, the first hero is a permission list. Use an approved first hero while you have one free; once those heroes are committed to other queues, send the remaining joins with no hero.

Do not fill an empty hero slot with a random hero. Only four joiner first skills are locked for the rally, and an unhelpful first skill can take one of those places. Supplying a selected skill does not give that player a private damage bonus; the skill is shared across the rally.
