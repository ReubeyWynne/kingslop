Run the hunt

  1. Poll before the event. Ask who's coming and whether they want the first or second group. Leave room for people who don't answer and people who turn up late.
  2. Save your formations. One for each joiner hero, one with no joiner hero, and one for each rally lead.

First heroes for joining

Gen I: Chenko, Yeonwoo, Amane, and Amadeus when you aren't leading with him.

Gen II–III: nothing new for joining. Hilde depends on your alliance; many ban her, so check your alliance's rule.

Gen IV: Margot, Rosa.

Gen V: Vivian.

Gen VI: Yang.

Later options: Petra (only one per rally), Ava, Wee & Woo.

  1. The first group launches straight away. With 15 players, that's 6 rallies. One more is fine if its leader doesn't mind cancelling.
  2. The second group launches between 29:00 and 28:30. Those rallies should still be waiting when the first group's joins get home. Leave enough time to hit the Bear, go home and join the next rally.
  3. If the first group doesn't fill, cancel a rally. Relaunch it with the second group instead of letting it go half-empty.
  4. After that, everyone relaunches as soon as they're home. While your rally marches home, open the rally screen and tap your saved formation. It won't load until your troops are back, so tap it again the moment they arrive.
  5. Look before you join. Tap into the rally. If it already has 4 joiner heroes, send your formation with no joiner hero.
  6. Lift the cap for the last 5 minutes. Anyone close to more rewards sends everything. Anyone who can't get more steps back.

Why it works

Each troop type's damage grows with roughly the square root of how many you send. Send twice the archers and you get about 41% more archer damage, not double.

The same troops, split across more marches:

1.00×
2 marches
1.41×
6 marches
2.45×

Each march in a rally is scored on its own, and its infantry, cavalry and archers each get their own square root. So the same troops do more damage spread across several joins than packed into one.

Exact model

For one march in a rally:

D ∝ KI√I + KC√C + KA√A

Each K covers the rally leader's attack and lethality, the troop's base attack, hero and widget bonuses, and the Bear matchup. Some skills only trigger by chance during the ten rounds, so the model uses their average.

Why 14, and why everyone launches

Say P players have six marches each, L of them launch every five minutes, and each rally has S joiners on average. Each march then hits H = L × S ÷ (P × 6) times every five minutes.

That's highest when L = P because everyone launches, and S = 14 because every rally fills. That gives H = 14 ÷ 6 = 2.33. With 13 joiners on average it drops to 2.17; with 14 joiners but only 80% of players launching, it's 1.87.

The 14th joiner adds damage to that rally, but the bigger gain is that returning marches reuse that space all event.

With fewer than 15 players, S can't go past P − 1, because a player joins each rally only once. Every five minutes, each player has P − 1 rallies to join, however many marches they send. About (P − 1) ÷ 2.33 joins reach all of them, so the rest of your troops can make those joins bigger, up to the cap. Damage grows with the square root of a join's troops, so fewer, bigger joins do more.

Why the cap

That's your biggest rally ÷ 16, which fits 14 full joins plus a double share for its leader, who is usually well ahead of everyone else and gets some extra damage from it.

Without it, a few big joins fill a rally's capacity and leave spaces empty. In the last five minutes, marches won't get home in time to join again, so the cap can go.

How many rallies in the first group?

Count the joins free at the start and divide by 14. 15 players with 6 joins each is 90, enough for 6 full rallies.

Once the first hits land, those marches come home and join again, so the size of the first group doesn't limit the rest of the event.
