BEAR HUNT

How Bear damage works and how to organise rallies around it.

30 min · every 48 hours · 5-minute rally cycle

The plan

The rally leader provides the combat stats and full hero kit. Each player's infantry, cavalry and archers are then scored separately. Up to four joiner first-hero skills can apply across the whole rally.

  • Everyone available should launch one rally per five-minute cycle. Stagger the launches so the rallies can fill.
  • Protect all 14 joiner slots whenever possible so returning join marches have as many scoring opportunities as possible.
  • Set a join-march cap that matches your alliance's rally capacity. Ours is about 85k right now; that number should change as rally capacities grow.
  • Keep your active join marches equal. Divide archers evenly first, then cavalry, then use infantry to finish each march. For a full march, 5 / 15 / 80 is a practical preset.
  • Use a recommended first hero on join marches. If none is available, send no hero.

Lead quality still matters: a stronger lead makes every hit in that rally stronger. The rest of the plan is about making sure the alliance creates enough useful rallies for its join queues to keep scoring.

For each troop type in your own march, damage grows roughly with the square root of the number you send. Doubling one troop-type count gives about 41% more damage from that type, not 100%.

Each player is calculated independently, and infantry, cavalry and archers are separate square-root terms inside that player's calculation. Splitting one player's available troops across several useful join queues creates more independent terms than concentrating them in one queue.

Exact model

For one participant, a useful proportional model is:

D ∝ KI√I + KC√C + KA√A

The K values include the rally leader's attack and lethality, troop base attack, hero and widget effects, and the Bear matchup. Chance-based skills vary across the ten rounds, so the planning model uses their average effect.

Aim for 14 joiners whenever possible. Full rallies give the alliance the most places to recycle its join queues.

With six join queues per player and one rally launch per player per five-minute cycle, fully filled rallies can support up to 2.33 Bear hits per join queue per cycle. Empty slots or players who do not launch reduce that ceiling.

Our alliance currently uses a cap of about 85k troops. That is an alliance-specific number, not a universal Bear rule, and it should rise as rally capacities grow. Its job is to stop oversized joins consuming enough rally capacity to keep another join march out.

The join cap does not apply to a strong lead's own rally march. Fill that march and use the lead's tested or simulated ratio. Their marches joining other rallies still follow the normal alliance cap.

Why 14 — and why everyone launches

Let P players each supply six join queues, let L of them launch in a five-minute cycle, and let S be the average number of joiners in each rally. The supported reuse is H = L × S ÷ (P × 6) hits per join queue.

The maximum needs both terms at their ceiling: L = P because everyone launches, and S = 14 because every rally fills. That gives H = 14 ÷ 6 = 2.33. Average 13 joiners and it falls to 2.17; only 80% of players launching with 14 joiners gives 1.87.

Filling the extra slot also improves that individual rally because another independently calculated march gets to hit. The wider benefit is that the slot can be reused by returning queues throughout the event.

Keep all active join marches the same size. Divide archers evenly first, then cavalry, then use infantry to fill the remaining space. When you have enough troops to fill every join march, 5 / 15 / 80 is the practical preset we use.

1. Equal marches first

Do not build one excellent join march and several weaker ones. You cannot reliably route a particular march to a particular rally without slowing the event down. Keep the queues even, and favour cavalry over infantry when filling the space left after archers.

2. Use 5 / 15 / 80 when full

For the rally leaders we have tested, 5% infantry / 15% cavalry / 80% archers has been very close to the exact mathematical optimum. The exact ratio still depends on the lead. At our current 85k cap, the preset is about 4.25k infantry, 12.75k cavalry and 68k archers per join march.

To field six 85k join marches at 5/15/80 at the same time, your account needs about 25.5k infantry, 76.5k cavalry and 408k archers available for those six queues. Until you have that depth, equal marches matter more than forcing the preset.

The allocator only handles the scarce archer step. Once those archers are split, add cavalry evenly across the join queues, then infantry, keeping every active join march the same total size. Prefer cavalry over infantry where you have a choice.

Everyone available should launch once per five-minute cycle — just not all at once. Open with two groups of rallies, then relaunch as soon as your rally heroes return.

  1. Poll before the event. Ask who will be available and whether they prefer Group 1 or Group 2. Leave some room for people who do not reply and for late arrivals.
  2. Group 1 launches immediately. Start enough rallies to give most opening join queues somewhere to go. A small surplus is fine if one or two leads are willing to cancel.
  3. Spread Group 2 launches across roughly 29:00–28:30 remaining. These rallies should still be gathering when Group 1 join marches return. Leave enough time for the hit, the return home and the march to the next rally.
  4. If Group 1 does not fill, cancel a flex rally. Relaunch it with Group 2 rather than letting it fire half-empty.
  5. After that, every launcher relaunches on return. Joiners take the next suitable rally that is close to firing. Travel time and normal player timing will naturally spread the later launches.

Fast relaunch: while your lead march is returning, open the rally screen and tap your saved Bear formation. The formation cannot load while those troops are away; tap it again as soon as they return to load it immediately.

How many rallies in Group 1?

Estimate how many join queues are available at the start, then divide by your target number of joiners per rally. With 15 players and six join queues each, 90 opening join queues support about six fully filled 14-joiner rallies, with six queues left for the next launch. Starting a seventh can still work if a flex lead is ready to cancel when the opening supply is thinner than expected.

After the first hits land, those join queues return and can be used again, so the opening-wave count does not limit the rest of the event.

On a join march, only the first hero can contribute to the rally's four shared joiner-skill slots. Use one of the recommended first heroes below. If none is available, send the march without a hero.

A random first hero can take one of those four slots with a defensive or otherwise unhelpful skill. If that hero is selected, the skill affects the whole rally, not just your march.

Recommended first heroes for join marches

Gen I: Chenko, Yeonwoo, Amane; Amadeus when he is not needed as your lead.

Gen II–III: no new default joiner hero. Hilde depends on alliance policy; many alliances do not allow her, so follow your alliance's rule.

Gen IV: Margot, Rosa.

Gen V: Vivian.

Gen VI: Yang.

Later options: Petra (avoid duplicate Petras in one rally), Ava, Wee & Woo.
