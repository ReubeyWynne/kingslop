BEAR HUNT

How Bear damage works and how to organise rallies around it.

30 min · every 48 hours · 5-minute rally cycle

The plan

The rally leader provides the combat stats and full hero kit. Each player's infantry, cavalry and archers are then scored separately. Up to four joiner first-hero skills can apply across the whole rally.

  • Launch enough rallies that returning join marches always have somewhere useful to go. Do not limit launches to only the strongest leads.
  • Set a join-march cap that matches your alliance's rally capacity. Ours is about 85k right now, which usually leaves room for 13–14 joiners.
  • For a full join march, use roughly 5% infantry / 15% cavalry / 80% archers. If you cannot field that in every active queue, split your scarce troops evenly instead of perfecting one march and weakening the rest.
  • Use a recommended first hero on join marches. If none is available, send no hero.

A stronger lead increases the damage of every march in that rally. More rallies give returning join marches more chances to score. The two goals work together: good leads make each hit stronger; enough rallies keep your queues hitting.

For each troop type in your own march, damage grows roughly with the square root of the number you send. Doubling one troop-type count gives about 41% more damage from that type, not 100%.

Each player is calculated independently, and infantry, cavalry and archers are separate square-root terms inside that player's calculation. Splitting one player's available troops across several useful join queues creates more independent terms than concentrating them in one queue.

Exact model

For one participant, a useful proportional model is:

D ∝ KI√I + KC√C + KA√A

The K values include the rally leader's attack and lethality, troop base attack, hero and widget effects, and the Bear matchup. Chance-based skills vary across the ten rounds, so the planning model uses their average effect.

Aim for 13–14 joiners per rally. Set your join-march cap to match your alliance's current rally capacity and progression.

Our alliance currently uses a cap of about 85k troops. That is an alliance-specific number, not a universal Bear rule, and it should rise as rally capacities grow.

The cap only manages shared rally capacity. It does not combine players' troops or damage. An oversized join march can simply consume enough capacity to exclude another independently calculated joiner.

The join cap does not apply to a strong lead's own rally march. Fill that march and use the lead's tested or simulated ratio. Their marches joining other rallies still follow the normal alliance cap.

Why 13–14?

For a fixed amount of one troop type under the same lead, spreading it across 14 otherwise equivalent participants instead of 10 gives about 18% more summed square-root output. Real rallies are not perfectly equal, but giving up several participant slots still costs damage.

5 / 15 / 80 is a per-march join preset. It is roughly optimal across a useful range of rally-leader coefficients; the exact mathematical optimum still depends on the lead.

1. Aim for 5 / 15 / 80

For each full join march, use about 5% infantry / 15% cavalry / 80% archers. At our current 85k cap, that is about 4.25k infantry, 12.75k cavalry and 68k archers in that march.

2. Equalise shortages

If you do not have enough troops to build that composition in every active join queue, do not make one perfect march and starve the others. Split the scarce troop type — usually archers — evenly across your queues, then fill the remaining space with cavalry and infantry.

To field six 85k join marches at 5/15/80 at the same time, your account needs about 25.5k infantry, 76.5k cavalry and 408k archers available for those six queues. Those totals are only an inventory check for your own simultaneous marches. They are not a rally-wide composition or a combined damage pool.

Open with two groups of rallies. After both groups are launched, relaunch as soon as your rally heroes return.

  1. Poll before the event. Ask who will be available and whether they prefer Group 1 or Group 2. Leave some room for people who do not reply and for late arrivals.
  2. Group 1 launches immediately. Start enough rallies to give most opening join queues somewhere to go. A small surplus is fine if one or two leads are willing to cancel.
  3. Group 2 launches with roughly 28:00–27:30 remaining. Group 1 join marches should be home in time to join them.
  4. If Group 1 does not fill, cancel a flex rally. Relaunch it with Group 2 rather than letting it fire half-empty.
  5. After that, relaunch on return. Joiners take the next suitable rally that is close to firing. Travel time and normal player timing will naturally spread the later launches.

Fast relaunch: while your lead march is returning, open the rally screen and tap your saved Bear formation. The formation cannot load while those troops are away; tap it again as soon as they return to load it immediately.

How many rallies in Group 1?

Estimate how many join queues are available at the start, then divide by your target number of joiners per rally. With 15 players and six join queues each, 90 opening join queues support about seven rallies with 13 joiners. Starting eight can still work if one or two leads are ready to cancel when needed.

After the first hits land, returning join queues can be used again, so later rallies are not limited by the opening count.

On a join march, only the first hero can contribute to the rally's four shared joiner-skill slots. Use one of the recommended first heroes below. If none is available, send the march without a hero.

A random first hero can take one of those four slots with a defensive or otherwise unhelpful skill. If that hero is selected, the skill affects the whole rally, not just your march.

Gen I: Chenko, Yeonwoo, Amane; Amadeus when he is not needed as your lead.

Gen II–III: no new default joiner hero. Hilde depends on alliance policy; many alliances do not allow her, so follow your alliance's rule.

Gen IV: Margot, Rosa.

Gen V: Vivian.

Later options: Petra (avoid duplicate Petras in one rally), Ava, Wee & Woo.
