The three online waves and the two HQ waves are where the event is won. The rest of the schedule is just the city rhythm in between.

Kills are points wherever your troops are standing — your city or somebody else's. That one fact decides most of the strategy:

Your own city pays a fixed wave reward no matter how many kills your garrison gets. So troops left at home aren't just idle — they're siphoning kills off the players who reinforced you. Send everyone out.

A reinforced city holding 200–300k of T8/T9 or higher survives every round, even at difficulty 11. Under that, ask your alliance to cover you — it's points for them too.

If you truly have more troops than all your marches can carry, leave the archers home — they kill the least in regular waves.

Resist the urge to heal between rounds. Healed troops pop back into your own city, right where they'll steal kills from whoever's covering you. If you really must, time it just before an HQ wave.

Before you launch, stack your deployment capacity — Marshal King perk, deployment gems, pet capacity buffs. Bigger marches mean more infantry in the garrison, and more infantry is more kills.

TRY IT — YOUR KILL SURFACE

3 garrisons score for you at once — 3× the kill surface of staying home.

This isn't a damage formula — there's no published one. It's the kill-surface idea: what decides your points per wave is your share of each garrison, not how many you're in.

Over-reinforcing cuts the other way: a garrison shares its kills proportionally, so a city that's already covered doesn't reward extra troops — the same kill pool just gets spread thinner. Keep using every queue, but point your marches at different players. The barometer is the troop count: ~200–300k of T8/T9 holds a city at difficulty 11, and anything past that is watering down someone's share.

The compounding, in one line

Five members who each send their marches into each other's cities defend the same total troops as before — but each of them now scores from five garrisons instead of one. Get the whole alliance doing it and the event's total output multiplies.

The garrison fires first, and the Vikings take hits in a fixed order: infantry, then cavalry, then archers. Get enough infantry in and the wave is over before archers get a turn.

In most garrisons, infantry and cavalry finish every regular wave before the archers' turn. Archers only start to matter late at high difficulty — around wave 12–13 at difficulty 11 — and on HQ wave 20.

Why archers wait

Vikings attack in a fixed order — infantry first, cavalry second, archers last — and the garrison shoots first. A garrison with enough infantry kills the whole wave in the first exchange and the archers never fire. Their kills only become real when the wave outlasts the infantry.

Waves 7, 14 and 17 only hit cities whose player is online when the wave fires. Go offline and it's not just your points that vanish — your reinforcers lose theirs too.

Every wave pays more than the last, and two of the three online waves sit in the back half, so the cost adds up fast. Set everything up perfectly and then log off, and you'll still struggle to crack the top 50. Leave the game open if you can, even if you're not really playing.

A city that falls once still gets the next wave. Fall twice and the Vikings stop coming — pull your troops and redeploy to a live city.

The event's Members tab shows who's online and how full their garrisons are — faster than asking in chat, and the easiest way to spot the gaps before wave 7.

The headquarters only gets attacked on waves 10 and 20, and it only takes 15 reinforcement marches. It's the one place where everyone's fighting over the same finite points.

Send mostly infantry — HQ kills score the same way, and infantry gets the turns. Work out your fair share (rally capacity ÷ 15) and don't exceed it: everyone wants a slot, and one over-stuffed march starves the wave of another's.

Don't camp troops there between waves. The points you'd pick up camping are less than the waves you'd miss at a city, and pulling out early can drop a march at your city exactly when wave 9 or 19 arrives. The HQ is a wave, not a parking spot.

Ground rules are an alliance call — first come, first served with a per-person cap, or a set rotation. The point is a rule that stops campers, because not everyone can fit.

Wave 20 at difficulty 11

The hardest wave in the event. Infantry often can't finish it alone, so archers finally get their turn — some alliances run a third archers at the top difficulty. Check your kill reports after each wave 20 and adjust.

If you keep losing it, look at the garrison lead: bear-hunt lethality heroes beat garrison/defence leads like Howard — the job is killing Vikings fast, not surviving.

Two jobs, two lists. Your own city is led by your guard station; the cities you reinforce run on the joiner leads you send.

HOME Your city — guard station

Keep your 3 strongest heroes in the guard station at all times. They lead your garrison and set the bonus for every reinforcer inside — don't send them out.

AWAY Reinforcing — joiner leads

Send bear-hunt offence heroes as your march leads. Attack and lethality kill Vikings faster, and kills are points. Garrison heroes like Howard or Gordon are built for surviving — the wrong job here.

Your other two hero slots in a march mostly add capacity — fill them with your next strongest.

A garrison uses the guard station's skills plus the first four unique joiner skills. Flat % buffs stack across joiners; chance-based procs don't — a second Jabel in the queue does nothing and blocks a slot a flat-buff hero could fill.

Why not Howard or Gordon

Howard and Gordon shine at outpost defence and PvP, where surviving matters. In Vikings your garrison fires first — you want lethality to end waves, not damage reduction. Gordon's best Vikings job is leading the HQ garrison as the bonus source, not joining a march.
